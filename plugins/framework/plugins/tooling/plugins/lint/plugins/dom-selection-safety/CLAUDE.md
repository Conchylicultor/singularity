# dom-selection-safety

Contributes the repo-wide `no-raw-selection-range` ESLint rule:
`<x>.getRangeAt(...)` is banned everywhere except the dom-selection primitive.

Read the document selection's range or rect through
[`@plugins/primitives/plugins/dom/plugins/dom-selection/web`](../../../../../../../primitives/plugins/dom/plugins/dom-selection/CLAUDE.md)
— `selectionRange()` for the range, `selectionRect()` for its bounding box.

## Why

`getRangeAt` is the one selection read that carries a **three-part guard**, and
the three parts fail at three different moments:

1. there may be no selection at all (`getSelection()` returns `null`),
2. a selection may carry no range (`rangeCount === 0`),
3. `getRangeAt(0)` itself **throws `IndexSizeError`** when the range was
   invalidated between the check and the read.

Four hand-rolled copies of this read existed in this repo and exactly **one**
had all three. The rest stopped at (1) and (2) and left (3) uncaught — a live
throw on a path nobody had exercised. That distribution is the signature of an
idiom nobody remembers whole: writing it correctly requires
recalling a failure mode that never shows up in the happy path. The primitive
states the guard once, so there is nothing left to remember.

## What stays legal

**Bare `getSelection()` is not banned.** `.toString()`, `.anchorNode`,
`.isCollapsed`, `.removeAllRanges()` and `.addRange(...)` are widely and
legitimately used, and none of them needs a guard — a missing selection is
`null` or `false`, never a throw. So is `document.createRange()`: constructing
your own Range is a different operation, and `page/editor`'s `nodeLineRect` does
exactly that. `getRangeAt` is the single read the rule owns.

## Sanctioned exception (on the `ignores` allowlist)

Exactly one entry: the primitive's own
`dom-selection/web/internal/dom-selection.ts`. All four prior callers —
caret-trigger's `caret-anchor.ts`, page-editor's `caret-geometry.ts` and
`format-toolbar-plugin.tsx`, and `diff-view.tsx` — were **migrated, not
exempted**. That includes `diff-view`, whose copy handler reads the range for
*content* rather than geometry and was otherwise a legitimate caller: a rule
that needs an allowlist entry for a correct use is enforcing less than it looks,
and migrating it handed that call site the missing `IndexSizeError` guard for
free.
