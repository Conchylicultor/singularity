# chord

A chord ear trainer: it plays a short loop of a real song and asks you to name
its chords, only ever choosing loops made of chords the learner has turned on.
The track page is `block-49ba706c-affe-417b-a9a1-b6873e8c7ea8` ("Chord trainer
app"); the design is `research/2026-09-16-apps-chord-trainer-song-index*.md`
(v1–v4).

The app itself (skeleton, training loop, progress) is
`research/2026-09-18-apps-chord-trainer-app.md`; its mockup is the prototype
`proto-1789461303-updb` (default options).

What exists today:

- `shell` — the app's identity (`chordApp`, id `chord`, `/chord`), its rail
  entry and frame (a thin header above the full-pane renderer), and its
  dark-only theme (`chordTheme`: the onyx blacks, the chord colours as
  `categorical-1…7`, Bodoni Moda numerals).
- `data-dirs` — the app's one data dir, `apps/chord/`. The song index keeps its
  snapshot in the `song-index` area.
- `song-index` — the index of TheoryTab sections and their 4-bar loop windows,
  loaded on first use, and the endpoints that pick loops; `SongIndexGate` shows
  the load until the index is ready. See its CLAUDE.md.
- `video-availability` — which videos still play in the embed (oEmbed's answer
  and the player's reports), so the loop query leaves the dead ones out.
- `vocabulary` — what the trainer says about a chord token (numeral, scale
  degree, function, answering key, piano voicing) and how one is drawn
  (`<ChordNumeral>`, `chordToneStyle`).
- `curriculum` — what the learner practises: each chord practised, heard or
  off, how much of a loop is blank, and how many other chords a loop may hold;
  and the catalog those chords are chosen from — every chord of the song index
  in tracks and sections, ordered by how often real songs use them. See its
  CLAUDE.md.
- `progress` — every checked round and its answers, the mastery rule, and the
  live `chord.progress` stats.
- `piano` — the app's piano: the notes it plays for a chord (bass doubled an
  octave below the voicing), the four-octave keyboard that draws them and plays
  under the finger, and the sound mix — the song and the piano as two
  channels, each on or off at its own level, the piano following the song's
  playhead. Always on screen — the old three-valued `reveal` setting is
  gone. See its CLAUDE.md.
- `trainer` — the round model and answer rules (`core`), and the trainer screen
  itself, the app's index pane (`web`).
