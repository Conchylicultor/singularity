# slider

The thin-track slider — a themed `<input type="range">`, 3px track and an 11px
thumb, the quiet secondary control rather than a form widget.

It exists because that look was **copied CSS**. The master volume in Sonata's
toolbar and the click volume in its metronome panel each carried their own
byte-identical forty lines of it (strip the UA appearance, paint the track, a
gradient fill for WebKit and a real `::-moz-range-progress` for Firefox, a focus
ring reached through two vendor pseudo-elements, a thumb that grows on hover),
and the per-track fader would have been the third copy.

Collapsing duplicated CSS is only half of it. Two of the decisions those copies
were making are decisions no call site should be making at all, and moving them
in here is what the primitive is actually for.

**The fill is normalized against the range.** Both copies wrote
`style={{"--fill": value * 100}}` inline. That is the right number only because
both their ranges happened to be 0–1 — correct twice by coincidence. The first
slider with any other range would have painted a filled portion that disagreed
with its own thumb, and nothing would have said so. The percentage is a function
of `(value, min, max)`, so it is computed in the one place all three are known,
and `min`/`max` are required rather than defaulted so the range is always
stated.

**The detent lives here.** A `detent` is a home position: a small tick on the
track, plus a magnetic zone that pulls a move landing near it onto it exactly.
It is what makes 100% findable on a fader that can be pushed past it. Drawing
that tick means positioning against the track's own geometry, and the geometry
is not what it looks like: a range input does not put its thumb at `value%` of
the track, it insets the travel by half a thumb at each end. So a tick at a
plain `value%` is off by up to five pixels — dead-on in the middle, worst at the
ends, which is the kind of error that looks like nothing until someone puts a
detent near an edge. The correction is a property of the mark layer (inset by
half a thumb on each side, so its box IS the thumb's travel) rather than
arithmetic repeated per mark. That is track knowledge, and it belongs to
whoever wrote the track, not to a feature that just wants a fader.

The magnet has one non-obvious rule, and it is the whole difference between a
detent and a trap: a move is only pulled home when it brings the value **closer**
to the detent. A zone wide enough to catch a pointer drag is several steps wide,
so without that rule an arrow key stepping away from the detent would land back
inside the zone and be dragged home again — a value that can be reached and never
left. Catching arrivals only means walking out is always allowed.

`step` is optional and means **continuous** when omitted (`step="any"`), not the
HTML default of 1: silently quantizing a 0–2 fader to three positions is not a
sane thing for an omitted prop to do.

`aria-label` is required. A bare range input has no accessible name, and nothing
about a naked track says what it is a level *of*.

The passthrough and `ref` land on the input, which is the control — where a
caller's `disabled`, `title`, pointer handlers or `data-*` selector target want
to be. `className` is routed to the wrapper instead, because the wrapper is the
box: it is what a caller is sizing when they write `w-24`. The wrapper is
rendered whether or not there is a detent, so that routing never moves.

```tsx
<Slider
  value={volume}
  min={0}
  max={2}
  step={0.01}
  detent={1}
  onValueChange={setVolume}
  aria-label="Track volume"
  className="w-24"
/>
```
