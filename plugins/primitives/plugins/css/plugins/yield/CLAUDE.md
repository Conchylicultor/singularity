# yield

The **yielding cell** — a box that falls below its own content width so a leaf
inside can ellipsize, and that never takes the row's slack. `min-w-0` (or
`min-h-0`), named once.

## The four space-sharing roles

A flex child answers two independent questions: **does it take slack?** and
**does it give below its own content?** All four answers now have a name.

| role | classes | axis? | the question it answers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [`rigidClass()`](../rigid/CLAUDE.md) | `shrink-0` | no | won't give at all |
| **`yieldClass(axis)`** | `min-w-0` \| `min-h-0` | **yes** | gives below its content, never takes slack |
| [`growClass()`](../grow/CLAUDE.md) | `flex-1` | no | takes slack, floors at its own content |
| [`fillClasses(axis)`](../fill/CLAUDE.md) | `flex-1 min-w-0` | yes | **= grow + yield** |
| *(no class — the default)* | — | — | gives down to its content, takes nothing |

`fillClasses` is not a fifth thing: it is literally `yieldClass(axis)` followed by
`growClass()`, and a test in `fill` pins that so the pair cannot drift from its
halves.

## Why `<Fill>` is wrong here, precisely

This is the mistake the primitive exists to prevent, and three files documented it
in prose before it had a name (`collapsible-card.tsx`, `workflow-tool-view.tsx`,
`breadcrumb.tsx`).

`Fill` emits `flex-1`, which is `flex: 1 1 0%` — **basis zero**. Put it on one of
two siblings that must both yield, and the layout engine resolves the fill'd cell
to 0 and hands the other sibling (basis `auto`) its full content width; the
deficit then comes out of the fill'd cell alone. The visible result is one cell
crushed to nothing beside a neighbour that never truncated.

Two cells that must **yield together** both take `yieldClass`. With basis `auto`
on both, the row's deficit is shared in proportion to their content sizes, which
is what "they truncate together" means mechanically.

## Take the class — there is no `<Yield>` component

`yieldClass(axis)` is the whole primitive. Every site in the corpus that wanted
this role already owned the box — a `<Stack>`, a `<Line>`, a `<Text>`, or a
component's `className` pass-through — so a wrapper would only add a box to hold
one class.

Nor is there a spacer idiom to claim: an **empty `<Fill>`** is already the
sanctioned growing spacer, and a box with no content has nothing to yield below,
so an empty yield box is a no-op by definition.

If a genuine wrapper site ever appears (children you cannot otherwise reach), add
the component then — the way `rigid` ships `<Rigid>` for exactly that shape.

## Axis, and why the family disagrees about it

`yieldClass` takes an axis because `min-width: 0` and `min-height: 0` are two
**different** properties and only one is right for a given container.
`rigidClass()` and `growClass()` take none because `flex-shrink` and `flex-grow`
are single properties that already apply along whichever axis the container
declared as its main axis. The disagreement is the design, not an oversight.
